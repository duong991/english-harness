# Study

Read `AGENTS.md`, `docs/WORKFLOW.md`, `docs/LEARNING_PATH.md`, and the authoritative learner files before choosing work. Read a recent session only if it materially helps choose the next task.

If a useful review is due, offer to do that first. If the learner explicitly asks for help starting English, or their practical profile is not yet established, use the `start` skill instead; it begins naturally with a short first sample and learning activity. Otherwise choose manageable material and one realistic task that is slightly above independent ability and matches the learner's priorities.

Do not assign an advanced work scenario by default to a learner without practical evidence. The `start` skill collects evidence through familiar, low-pressure tasks; it is not a formal assessment.

For genuinely new language, first give a small, useful input: usually three to eight chunks from the material, with current sense and only the pronunciation, collocation, or register detail that supports use. Then close the source and ask the learner to recognise, retrieve, say, or write it in a related situation. Do not call explained input learner evidence.

Choose the relevant recipe in `docs/LEARNING_PATH.md`: listening uses audio and a reliable transcript; reading begins with gist before lookup; vocabulary moves toward closed-source use; speaking develops from familiar answers and follow-ups; writing develops from a clear audience and purpose. A session may connect several skills around one material, but do not force every skill into every session.

Match the task to the learner's intended channel. For speaking practice, prefer 30–90 second turns, meeting-style responses, follow-up questions, clarification, and repair. Do not turn speaking practice into an essay merely because text is easier to record. Use word counts only when they help the chosen task; a brief realistic instruction is usually enough.

For retrieval or performance, ask for self-noticing after the attempt. Then give at most two or three high-value corrections, quoting learner evidence. Prefer a concise hint, natural chunk, or question that lets the learner repair the response. Do not rewrite the answer.

Require a learner-authored retry. Compare the two versions briefly, emphasizing what the learner can now do rather than claiming that AI-assisted quality is independent proficiency.

For a meaningful session, create a dated Markdown record following `docs/WORKFLOW.md`. Keep the original attempt intact. Update progress, active vocabulary, errors, and reviews only when there is durable evidence or a useful next action; do not manufacture bookkeeping.
