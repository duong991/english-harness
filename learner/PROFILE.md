# Learner Profile

## Goal

Not established yet.

Use `start` to identify the main purpose for English before choosing a learning path. Record an observable outcome, intended context, and (when useful) a time horizon. The purpose can be work, study, an exam, travel, relocation, relationships, media, a hobby, or a combination; do not assume a profession or domain.

## Primary situations

Not established yet.

Record the few situations that matter most: who the learner communicates with, what they need to understand or do, and whether the channel is listening, reading, speaking, writing, or a mix.

## Current ability

Not established yet.

Begin with goal discovery and lightweight placement when the learner asks for help starting English. Build a practical, separate picture of listening, reading, speaking, writing, and receptive/productive vocabulary from short raw samples; this can happen across more than one session. Run a formal assessment only when the learner explicitly asks for one, then update this section with a practical description rather than a claimed certification level.

## Priorities

Not established yet.

After goal discovery and initial evidence, rank the learner's priority skills and constraints. Do not assume speaking, a work domain, or equal time across all four skills.

## Preferences

Not established yet.

Record preferences that materially affect lesson design, such as preferred topics, feedback style, available time, comfort with recordings, and materials to avoid.
